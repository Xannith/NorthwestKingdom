Reshard Lane: pole and access

A power pole stands within the recorded easement near Kevin Reimer's driveway. Together with posts set along the lane, it restricts passage for large passenger vehicles, trucks, and emergency vehicles. The recorded easements along this route are ten feet wide, which is a documented constraint independent of the pole itself.

April 16, 2026 — Prescriptive easement discussion circulated

Informal research Source: message from Dee Ann Maxfield, forwarded by Leslie Reimer, subject “Fw: Prescriptive easement.”

The forwarded material raised the possibility that a utility company may hold a prescriptive easement where poles stood long before a recorded easement existed. The general Florida requirements discussed were continuous and uninterrupted use for twenty years, use that is adverse rather than permissive, use that is open and notorious, a definite and identifiable route, and possible “tacking” of successive owners' periods of use.

This is neighbor research, not a legal opinion, and no determination has been made as to whether any of it applies to Reshard Lane.

Known gaps in the record

A citation in the 2003 instrument does not resolve. Page 3 of Exhibit A cites the Reimer easements at “O.R. book 1446, page 608–615 and O.R. book 1446, page 625–626.” The first citation is correct and is detailed above. The second is not: page 625 returns no recording, and page 626 is an unrelated condominium deed between parties with no connection to Northwest Kingdom. Do not repeat the 625–626 citation as though it points to a Reimer easement until the intended reference is identified.
  • Part of the Coop grant is redacted. In the Clerk's web image of OR 1446/612, the phrase identifying which northern edge the ten-foot strip runs along is obscured. A certified copy from the Clerk's office would be needed to read it.
  • The chain of title after 1990 has not been traced. The five easements run to Kevin Reimer and Nancy H. Reimer by name. Whether and how they bind or benefit current owners depends on deeds recorded since, which are not summarized here.
  • Dee Ann Maxfield is not named in the 2003 instrument. It runs to the Lambs. Correspondence indicates she acquired the parcel from them in 2005 and that the covenants bind successors, but the intervening deed has not been pulled.
  • The attorney's first notice to Kevin Reimer has not been located. Searches of the available correspondence did not turn up a copy. It should not be described as found or reviewed unless the actual document is obtained.
  • Two Heinlen parties appear in the 2003 instrument. Winonna Heinlen and S. Elizabeth Heinlen are listed as separate grantors holding separate lots. Whether “Betsi Heinlen” in current correspondence is S. Elizabeth Heinlen has not been confirmed from a recorded document.

How to verify these records

Every recorded item above is public. To pull an original, use the Leon County Clerk's official records search, set Book Type to Official Records, and enter the Book and Page from the citations above.

  • Leon County Official Records Search — free to search and view document images.
  • Leon County Clerk — Official Records information — copy fees, office locations, and how to request certified copies.

Note: the search indexes an instrument by its first page, so searching an interior page such as 609 or 613 returns no result even though the page is part of a recorded document. Images shown by the portal are marked “unofficial”; certified copies must be requested from the Clerk.

Correspondence sources. Neighbor emails are cited above by author, date, and subject rather than by link, because the available links point into a single private mailbox and will not open for other members. If you need the underlying message, contact the Records Steward.
