NWK Fire Ring Rules

Rules for use of the Northwest Kingdom fire ring.

The fire ring is a shared resource. These rules exist so that we keep it, and so that we do not put our neighbors or the surrounding woods at risk.

This page covers the NWK fire ring only. The MLC fire ring on common land is a separate facility with its own Town Council policy. See the MLC Bonfire Policy for that one. Neither set of rules replaces the other.

Before you light a fire

  1. Check for a burn ban. If Leon County has a burn ban in effect, the fire ring is closed. County bans prohibit campfires, fire pits, and similar recreational fires on private property, with no exception for community land. Check the Leon County and Florida Forest Service notices, not just the weather.
  2. Do not light a fire during drought conditions, a red flag warning, or high wind. If it is dry and gusty, wait for a better night.
  3. Have water on site before the first match. A charged hose, filled buckets, or a full extinguisher, plus a shovel or rake. Set it up first, not after.
  4. Clear the ring. Rake back leaves, branches, and other loose fuel from around the ring, and check for low overhanging limbs.

While the fire is burning

  1. Never leave a fire unattended. An adult stays with the fire from lighting to cold out. If you have to leave, put it out first.
  2. Keep it small. Fires stay inside the ring. No fuel piled higher than the ring, and no fires you cannot put out with the water on hand.
  3. Burn clean wood only. No trash, no treated or painted lumber, no plastic, no construction debris, and no accelerants such as gasoline.
  4. Keep children and pets back from the ring, and keep an adult between them and the fire.

When you are done

  1. Put it out completely. Douse with water, stir the ashes, and douse again. Repeat until the ashes are cool to the touch. Cool to the touch is the standard, not “it looks out.”
  2. Leave the ring better than you found it. Pack out bottles, cans, and food waste, and restock any wood you used if that is the arrangement.
If a fire escapes the ring, call 911 first. Then notify a neighbor. Do not spend time trying to handle it quietly.
